What you're seeing

Coloured areas are neighbourhoods (ABS SA2s, about suburb size). The colour shows the measure chosen above, for the year and sea level chosen at the top of the page.

Blue: planning-scheme flood overlays (LSIO, FO for rivers; SBO for overland flow): land councils have mapped as flood-prone.

Teal: land under the sea at the chosen sea level, calm or with a 1-in-100-year storm tide (Victorian Coastal Inundation Dataset, a "bathtub" model).

Names mark the five places (three on a phone) with the highest values on the current view.

Living in a flood-prone area is exposure, not damage: newer buildings must sit above the flood level, and most apartment residents live above it. Tap a neighbourhood for its numbers.

How accurate is the placement?real result

Error is WAPE: the total absolute error divided by total residents. "Within" is the share of units within 10 people or 20% of the Census count. SA2 totals were set to the true 2021 values, so this measures placement only; real forecasts add the error of the totals themselves. On this evidence the page shows suburbs and SA2s as headline figures, SA1s only with ranges, and never single mesh blocks.

Method

  • Starting point: ABS Estimated Resident Population by SA2, mid-2025.
  • Council totals: Victoria in Future 2023 growth from mid-2025, added to the actual 2025 population (VIF was within 0.6% of the 2025 total, but slightly short on growth).
  • Where growth goes: new homes in the Urban Development Program (major redevelopment projects and greenfield land, weighted by how likely each is to be built by then), capped at each council's projected new dwellings. The remaining small infill follows where addresses were created in the last five years. Tested on past years: 2.7–6% error over 4–5 years, 17% over 14 years, much higher in small and fast-growing areas.
  • Today's flood areas: the planning-scheme flood overlays (LSIO, FO, SBO) plus the present-day coastal inundation extent. Existing residents are counted by where address points fall; new residents by where homes built since 2016 fall in the same area.
  • Sea-level rise: the Victorian Coastal Inundation Dataset's +0.20, +0.47 and +0.82 m extents (2040, 2070, 2100). It is a bathtub model, made for 1:75,000 scale, and not suitable for single properties. Shown here with 2036 population: the population is held at each year's forecast, and the sea level is a what-if.
  • Ranges: from measured past error at each horizon and for each SA2's size and growth class; council and total ranges from VIF's measured error, growing with the horizon.
